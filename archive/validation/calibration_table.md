Rows marked *naive foil* are the methods this package replaces; they are here to show what goes wrong, not as recommendations. *Reference* is a method shown for comparison. Rows where a recommended method is outside its band are reported as they came out.

**Reading the bands.** A calibrated method still lands outside its 2-SE band about 1 time in 20, and this table has 25 simulated rows, so about 1.2 such rows are expected by chance. Seeds are those of the slow tests (`pytest -m slow`), which check the same scenarios. While preparing this table, CUPED with the curved covariate came out at 5.7% under an unrelated seed; over 16 seeds (that one included) of 10,000 replications each it rejects 4.97% of the time.

**Small N.** The count of correct answers is binomial, so coverage of an interval for an accuracy can be computed exactly, and it is a sawtooth in the true accuracy: one accuracy can look comfortably conservative while another is well below nominal. Hence the worst case over accuracies from 0.02 to 0.98. Only Clopper-Pearson is guaranteed to cover at least 95% everywhere; Wilson and Jeffreys are close on average but dip below nominal in places.
