# Step 5: power and sample size (2026-11-13)

Script `analysis/05_power.py`, seed 20261113. Results: `results/05_sample_size.csv`,
`results/05_power_checks.csv`, chart `results/05_power.png`.
Baseline = real no-email buying rate 0.5726%. Test: two-sided z-test, alpha 5%, 10,000 fake
experiments per simulated case. User chose 0.3 points as the smallest lift worth detecting.

Formula (pooled variance under no effect, unpooled under a real effect) written by hand; matches
statsmodels `samplesize_proportions_2indep_onetail` and `power_proportions_2indep` to 1e-15. That
only shows the code is right (same formula), so the independent check is simulation.

## People per group (80% power | 90% power)
| True lift (points) | 80% | 90% |
|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | 97,126 | 130,024 |
| 0.15 | 44,888 | 60,092 |
| 0.20 | 26,217 | 35,097 |
| **0.30** | **12,511** | 16,749 |
| 0.50 | 5,122 | 6,856 |
| 0.68 | 3,069 | 4,108 |
Halving the lift from 0.30 to 0.15 raises the need 3.6 times (not exactly 4: the variance also changes).

## Simulation check
- At each formula n, simulated power is 79.5% to 81.1%. Within about 1 point of 80%; slightly above
  at the larger lifts (discrete counts), so the formula is a good approximation, not exact.
- True lift 0.3 points at fixed n: 2,000 -> 20% power; 5,000 -> 43%; 10,000 -> 71%; 21,300 -> 96%.

## Winner's curse
Among experiments that came out significant, the average estimated lift divided by the true lift:
2.2x at 2,000 per group (20% power), 1.5x at 5,000 (43%), 1.2x at 10,000 (71%), about 1.12x even at
80% power, 1.03x at 96%. Significant results from underpowered tests exaggerate.

## Our experiment (about 21,300 per group)
- Smallest lift detectable with 80% power: 0.224 points; with 90%: 0.263.
- Power if the true lift were 0.10 / 0.15 / 0.20 / 0.25 / 0.30 / 0.50 points: 26% / 49% / 71% / 87% /
  96% / 100%. So a true 0.3-point lift was very likely to be caught; lifts below about 0.2 likely missed.

## Predictions vs result
- People for a 0.3-point lift: predicted 5,000 to 20,000. Correct (12,511).
- Halving the lift: predicted about 4 times as many. Correct (3.6 times).

## Limits
- Assumes independent customers and a fixed baseline of 0.57%; the real baseline is uncertain.
- Planning for the lift you hope for is a business choice; do not compute power from an observed
  lift after the fact (it just restates the p-value).
- Covers the z-test for purchase rate only; spend and visits need their own planning.
